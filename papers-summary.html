CORAL: Towards Autonomous Multi-Agent Evolution for Open-Ended Discovery

arXiv: 2604.01658  |  GitHub: Human-Agent-Society/CORAL  |  File: coral_multi_agent_evolution_open_ended_discovery_2604.01658.pdf    AgentsOptimization

CORAL is a framework that lets LLM agents autonomously run open-ended evolutionary search — continuously improving solutions to hard optimization problems without a human specifying the search strategy.

Three Evolutionary Paradigms

ParadigmWho controls the search?
Fixed evolutionary search (baseline)Human-designed algorithm
Autonomous single-agent evolutionOne LLM agent
Autonomous multi-agent evolution (CORAL)Multiple agents co-evolving via shared memory

Architecture

  • Shared Persistent Memory: structured filesystem (attempts/, notes/, skills/) readable and writable by all agents
  • Asynchronous Multi-Agent Coordination: agents work in isolated workspaces but coordinate organically via shared artifacts — no rigid message-passing
  • Heartbeat Mechanism: periodic prompts for reflection, knowledge consolidation, and stagnation-breaking
3–10× higher improvement rate over fixed evolutionary baselines across 11 tasks; converges in 5–20 evaluations vs. 60–100 for baselines. New SOTA on 10 tasks including Kernel Engineering (1,363 → 1,103 cycles).

Why It Works

  • Cross-agent transfer: 36% of high-performing attempts build on another agent's work (17% improvement rate vs. 9% average)
  • Exploration diversity: Jaccard similarity ~0.43 — more than half of each agent's strategy vocabulary is unique
  • Local verification: testing code locally before formal evaluation yields ~23 pp advantage on compiled-code tasks
  • Gains are structural, not from extra compute — independent parallel runs are weaker than true co-evolution
Limitations: relies on frontier LLMs; homogeneous agent initialisation; assumes well-specified evaluators

DeepSeek-V4: Towards Highly Efficient Million-Token Context Intelligence

Organization: DeepSeek-AI  |  Models: HuggingFace  |  File: deepseek-v4-technical-report.pdf    LLMArchitectureLong-Context

DeepSeek-V4 is a family of two Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models designed to efficiently process one-million-token contexts by dramatically reducing the compute and memory cost of long-context attention.

Models

ModelTotal ParamsActivated ParamsContext
DeepSeek-V4-Pro1.6T49B1M tokens
DeepSeek-V4-Flash284B13B1M tokens

Key Architectural Innovations

  • Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA): compresses KV caches along the sequence dimension, then performs sparse attention — reduces KV cache dramatically at long contexts
  • Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA): applies more aggressive KV compression while keeping dense attention — further cuts memory at the cost of some expressivity
  • Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC): upgrades conventional residual connections by constraining the residual mapping matrix to the Birkhoff polytope (doubly stochastic matrices), stabilising signal propagation
  • Muon Optimizer: replaces Adam for faster convergence and improved training stability
At 1M-token context, DeepSeek-V4-Pro requires only 27% of single-token inference FLOPs and 10% of KV cache compared to DeepSeek-V3.2. V4-Flash achieves 10% FLOPs and 7% KV cache.

Post-Training Pipeline

Two-stage: (1) domain-specific expert training (SFT + GRPO RL per domain: math, code, agent, instruction-following); (2) unified model consolidation via on-policy distillation from the specialists.

Results

  • Knowledge: leads open-source models on SimpleQA; closes gap with Gemini-3.1-Pro but still trails on knowledge benchmarks
  • Reasoning: outperforms GPT-5.2 and Gemini-3.0-Pro on standard reasoning; ~3–6 months behind GPT-5.4 and Gemini-3.1-Pro
  • Agent: competitive with Kimi-K2.6 and GLM-5.1; outperforms Claude Sonnet 4.5, approaches Opus 4.5
  • Long-context: surpasses Gemini-3.1-Pro on academic long-context benchmarks
Note: This is a preview release; training on 32–33T diverse tokens.

Evaluating the Capabilities of LLMs for Persuasive Dialogue

Authors: Jordan Robinson, Angus R. Williams, Katie Atkinson, Anthony G. Cohn  |  University of Liverpool / Alan Turing Institute / University of Leeds  |  File: Evaluating_the_Capabilities_of_LLMs_for_Persuasive_Dialogue.pdf    EvaluationArgumentation

This paper asks: does sounding persuasive mean arguing well? It introduces PERSUASIO, a multi-agent debate platform grounded in formal argumentation theory, and finds a systematic gap between rhetorical fluency and logical soundness in LLMs.

Setup

  • Platform implements Prakken (2006) formal persuasion dialogue system via LangGraph
  • 192 debate transcripts on UK political topic across human-human, human-model, model-model configurations
  • 22 interlocutors: 13 humans, 9 LLM variants (GPT-4o, Grok-3, Mistral-Medium in BASE/MAS/MAS+RAG modes)
  • Persuasiveness measured on two axes: logical (automated adjudication via formal dialogue rules) and subjective (9,702 crowdsourced pairwise judgements)

Key Finding: Rhetoric vs. Rigour Decoupling

All 9 LLM configurations occupied the top 9 positions in subjective persuasiveness, yet showed substantial rank inversions under logical evaluation — where humans remained competitive (a human debater ranked #1 logically). MAS and RAG enhancements widened this gap.

What This Means

  • LLMs are optimised for rhetorical polish (fluency, structural clarity, confident framing) rather than formal argumentative rigour
  • RLHF/instruction tuning rewards rhetorical appeal, which may mask logical weaknesses
  • Multi-agent orchestration and RAG amplify subjective persuasiveness without reliably improving logical coherence
  • Demographic subgroups (age, gender, political leaning) all closely reproduce the subjective ranking, but diverge strongly from the logical one — suggesting subjective judgements are stable but systematically misleading
Implication: Benchmarks relying on human preference risk conflating persuasive appeal with argumentative validity, incentivising surface-level polish over reasoning quality.

The Long-Horizon Task Mirage? Diagnosing Where and Why Agentic Systems Break

Authors: Xinyu Jessica Wang et al.  |  University of Wisconsin–Madison, UC Berkeley, Georgia Tech  |  arXiv: 2604.11978  |  File: long-horizon-task-mirage.pdf    AgentsBenchmarkFailure Analysis

LLM agents perform well on short tasks but break down systematically as task horizons grow. This paper introduces HORIZON, a cross-domain diagnostic benchmark, and a 7-category failure taxonomy to characterise where and why agents fail at long horizons.

HORIZON Benchmark

  • 4 domains: Web navigation, OS, Embodied, Database
  • Tasks are parameterised by Intrinsic Horizon H* (minimum optimal actions) and Compositional Depth s (nested sub-goals)
  • 3,100+ trajectories from GPT-5 variants and Claude-4 models
  • LLM-as-a-Judge pipeline for scalable failure attribution (inter-annotator κ=0.61; human–judge κ=0.84)

7-Category Failure Taxonomy

CategoryDescription
Environment DisturbanceAgent fails to detect or adapt to environmental changes
Instruction FailureMisinterprets or fails to follow instructions
False AssumptionsActs on incorrect beliefs about state or data
Planning ErrorFlawed sub-plan or action ordering
Catastrophic ForgettingLoses track of earlier constraints as horizon grows
History Error AccumulationEarly mistakes compound into trajectory-level failure
Memory LimitationContext window overflow causes loss of critical info
Performance degrades non-linearly with horizon and exhibits a sharp cliff beyond small compositional depth s. Planning-related and memory-related failures become dominant at long horizons — suggesting scaling base models alone is insufficient.

Key Findings

  • Performance gaps between models narrow substantially once the breaking region is entered — long-horizon failure levels the playing field
  • The same extension level s induces markedly different difficulty across domains (Web collapses early; Database sustains longer)
  • Method-level improvements in planning, memory, and execution-time control are needed beyond base-model scaling

Parcae: Scaling Laws for Stable Looped Language Models

Authors: Hayden Prairie, Zachary Novack, Taylor Berg-Kirkpatrick, Daniel Y. Fu  |  UC San Diego / Together AI  |  arXiv: 2604.12946  |  File: parcae-scaling-laws-looped-llm.pdf    ArchitectureScaling LawsLooped LLM

Looped (recurrent-depth) architectures can scale FLOPs without increasing parameters, but prior work was unstable. Parcae diagnoses instability via control theory and proposes a stable looped transformer with principled scaling laws.

Why Looped Models Are Unstable

Parcae recasts the looped forward pass as a Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) dynamical system over the residual stream. Instability occurs when the spectral radius ρ(A) ≥ 1 in the injection parameter matrix — prior looped architectures (addition-based, concatenation-based) are marginally stable or unconstrained.

Parcae's Solution

  • Parameterises A as a negative diagonal matrix (via Diag(-exp(log_A))), guaranteeing ρ(A) < 1
  • Normalises the input injection B to prevent late-stage loss spikes
  • Introduces per-sequence depth sampling to stabilise variable-depth training
Parcae achieves 6.3% lower validation perplexity than prior large-scale looped models. A 770M Parcae matches a 1.3B Transformer on Core benchmarks with roughly half the parameters.

Scaling Laws of Looping

  • Training: looping is an orthogonal scaling axis — compute-optimal training increases both looping and data following empirical power laws
  • Test-time: additional recurrence follows predictable saturating exponential decay; scaling test-time looping is predictable and converges
  • At 1.3B params, Parcae improves Core by 2.99 pts and Core-Extended by 1.18 pts over matched Transformers

Scaling Latent Reasoning via Looped Language Models (Ouro)

Authors: Rui-Jie Zhu, Zixuan Wang et al.  |  ByteDance Seed, UC Santa Cruz, Princeton, Mila, CMU, Yoshua Bengio  |  arXiv: 2510.25741  |  Project: ouro-llm.github.io  |  File: scaling-latent-reasoning-looped-llm.pdf    ArchitectureReasoningLooped LLM

Instead of reasoning through explicit chain-of-thought text, Ouro builds reasoning into the pre-training architecture via looped computation in latent space — a third scaling axis beyond model size and training data.

Key Ideas

  • LoopLM Architecture: a fixed-depth transformer block is applied T times, with shared weights. An exit gate at each step outputs a probability of terminating early
  • Entropy-Regularized Adaptive Computation: training objective = expected task loss + entropy regularization over the exit distribution. This prevents collapse to always using Tmax steps while allowing easy inputs to exit early
  • Stage II Focused Gate Training: freeze LM, fine-tune only the exit gate to balance marginal improvement vs. compute cost
  • Trained on 7.7T tokens to produce Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B (and Thinking variants via Reasoning SFT)
Ouro-1.4B matches 4B standard transformers; Ouro-2.6B matches 8B models — 2–3× parameter efficiency. Ouro-2.6B-Thinking R4 matches or exceeds Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-7B on math/science benchmarks.

Why LoopLM Gains Are Real

  • Recurrence does not increase raw knowledge storage (≈2 bits/param for both looped and non-looped) — it improves knowledge manipulation (fact composition, multi-hop reasoning)
  • LoopLM produces reasoning traces more aligned with final outputs than CoT, indicating greater causal faithfulness
  • Safety improves monotonically with recurrent steps on HEx-PHI
