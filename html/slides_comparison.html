Analysis

What Reality Revealed About Each Model's Blind Spots

Gemma's Blind Spot

Underestimated institutional confidence. Gemma bet that transparency would cause panic. Singapore bet the opposite — and won. PM-led public communication stabilised sentiment, while stealth would have created an information vacuum exploited by misinformation.

GPT OSS's Blind Spot

Missed the economic relief dimension. GPT OSS correctly predicted the transparency and multilateral posture — but entirely missed the ~S$1B direct fiscal relief package, MAS monetary tightening, and IEA coordination. It modelled process without modelling household-level impact.

Qwen 3.5's Blind Spot

Overestimated Singapore's willingness to break rules. Shadow Fleet, SWRIF bypass, and covert procurement assumed Singapore would sacrifice its rule-of-law reputation for survival. Reality: Singapore stayed conspicuously legal and used that as diplomatic leverage.

What All Models Got Wrong

  • None predicted the S$1B fiscal relief package or MAS tightening
  • None predicted IEA emergency oil release participation
  • None modelled aviation sector response (SAF levy deferral)
  • All overestimated crisis duration / severity of domestic supply shock

What All Models Got Right

  • Energy diversification away from Gulf — correct direction
  • MPA / maritime monitoring elevated — anticipated
  • JS-SEZ / Malaysia bilateral importance — all flagged
  • No military deployment or reservist recall — all predicted correctly
  • Malacca Strait as strategic concern — all flagged

Conclusion

Takeaways: Prediction vs. Reality

Reality chose the GPT OSS doctrine — structured transparency, multilateral rule-of-law diplomacy, calibrated visible defence signaling, and legal energy diversification. Singapore's actual posture was more open, more institutionally confident, and more fiscally activist than any model anticipated.

GPT OSS · Best Predictor

Correctly identified: UNSC multilateralism, transparency over stealth, calibrated alert without deployment, legal procurement channels, and rule-of-law as leverage.

Qwen 3.5 · Right Risks, Wrong Response

Correctly identified the US security gap risk and Malacca spillover concern — but concluded Singapore would go covert. It didn't. Legal visibility was the chosen shield.

Gemma · Inverted Reality

The "Narrative Lock" and stealth posture were the mirror image of what happened. Singapore's government communicated loudly and clearly — because credibility required it.

The Universal Gap: Economic Statecraft

All three models focused on crisis management — stockpiles, defence posture, narrative control. None anticipated the proactive fiscal and monetary response: S$1B household support, MAS tightening, IEA coordination, aviation levy deferral. Singapore treated the crisis as an opportunity to demonstrate institutional competence — not just to survive it.
