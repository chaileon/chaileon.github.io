Why these comparisons are weak

Protocol mismatch is the rule, not the exception. MalayMMLU's leaderboard uses first-token accuracy; ours is generative CoT. Vendor figures for Qwen3.8-27B and GLM-5.2 state no k, temperature or thinking budget at all. None of these were reproduced independently.

Our numbers carry a penalty theirs do not. The 40,960-token cap scores non-termination as failure. No published figure does this — which is why the “excl. capped” column exists, and why the honest reading of a7n sits between the two columns.

Sources contradict each other. GLM-5.2's GPQA appears as 85.4, 89.5 and 91.2 depending on source — and 85.4 is actually GLM-5-FP8, a different model, conflated in one summary. Its 99.1% τ² figure is not credible on a benchmark far from saturation.

Baselines age badly. IndoMMLU's published figures are from its 2023 paper, when GPT-3.5 led at 53.2. Beating them by 20 points says more about three years of progress than about this model specifically.

Five benchmarks have no usable published counterpart at matching settings: AIME 2024, MATH-500, IFEval, SimpleQA and BFCL v4. They are reported here without comparison rather than against a mismatched number.

How to read this table — and how not to

a7n's two columns bracket the truth. The first applies the 40,960-token cap as a failure; the second removes it entirely. Published figures apply no such penalty, so the right-hand number is the fairer comparison — but it assumes every non-terminating sample would have been correct, which is an upper bound, not a score.

The AIME years do not match. We ran AIME 2025; Qwen3.5 and MiniMax report AIME 2026. Those columns are not comparable and are labelled accordingly.

Zhipu published no official benchmarks when GLM-5.2 shipped. Every GLM-5.2 number in circulation is third-party, which is why its GPQA appears variously as 85.4, 89.5 and 91.2 — and why one widely-repeated figure is actually GLM-5-FP8, a different model.

Most cells are empty because the data does not exist at matching settings, not because the models were not tested. MMLU-Pro in particular is rarely reported for this class in 2026 — it has largely been displaced by SWE-bench and Terminal-Bench in vendor reporting.

Active parameters matter more than totals for inference cost. These models activate 17–55B per token despite 397B–1T totals, so “200B–1T MoE” describes memory footprint, not compute per token.

Qwen3.6's own subject spread shows the benchmark's structure. Academic subjects sit at 80–100%; local-language subjects collapse. IndoMMLU is roughly half local-language and cultural content, which is what pulls both models down from their English scores.

Qwen3.6-27B had zero unparsed and zero truncated samples, against a7n's 1.1% and 1.0%. On a benchmark whose expected answer is a single letter, a7n still occasionally reasons past the 40,960-token cap and emits nothing; a dense 27B never did.

Sample sizes differ. Qwen ran n=348–350 per benchmark against a7n's 1,500–2,429, because Qwen3.6-27B is a paid model at $3.60/M output. Matching a7n's sample sizes would have cost roughly $59.

Size context. Qwen3.6-27B is a dense 27B model, far smaller than the 200B–1T MoE class tabled above. That a model this size leads a7n on Indonesian is the substantive finding here.

Method & caveats

Self-judging corrupted one headline number and was corrected. SimpleQA originally scored 19.1% correct / 49.9% not attempted with a7n-m5-v1 as its own grader. An independent Qwen3.8-27B judge gives 24.9% / 17.8%, and the characterisation reverses entirely — from “abstains often” to “answers confidently and is wrong 53.9% of the time”. The τ² user simulator, τ²'s NL-assertion checker and WildChat refusal labelling remain self-judged and carry the same risk.

Thinking cannot be disabled. Tested three ways with repeats: thinking:{"type":"disabled"} produced more reasoning (470 chars) than sending no parameter (251). The endpoint ignores it. Every figure here is a thinking-mode figure and is not comparable to a non-thinking baseline.

An endpoint bug empties some responses. Roughly 134 SimpleQA samples returned stop_reason: tool_use with empty text despite no tools parameter being sent. Those count as failures and are not model refusals.

GPQA uses an ungated mirror. Idavidrein/gpqa is gated, so this run uses fingertap/GPQA-Diamond — the same 198 Diamond items in four-choice form, answers near-uniform across A–D.

Scoring uses official verifiers. IFEval runs the google-research verifier vendored unchanged; math answers are graded by math_verify; LiveCodeBench solutions execute against full public and private test suites under sandbox-exec with the network denied; BFCL and τ²-bench run their own harnesses. Regional MCQ prompts are written in Indonesian and Malay so the model is not cued into English.

Not run: SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench and OSWorld all need per-task Docker containers, unavailable on this machine. SEA-HELM is paused at 4 of 18 task-language combinations. IndoCulture is still running.

Nemotron comparison covers MMLU-Pro only. OpenRouter's free tier enforces a ~1,000 request/day cap. Recovering MMLU-Pro's 504 rate-limited samples consumed the day's quota, so all four regional MCQ runs returned 100% rate-limit failures — their summaries were quarantined rather than reported, since a “0.0%” built entirely from HTTP 429s is not a measurement. SEA-HELM on Nemotron was abandoned after 4h42m produced zero completed tasks.
